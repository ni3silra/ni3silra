==============

While working on the legacy systems, I realized that every legacy system has one problem: visibility, or I would say limited visibility. When it comes to the db Internet platform, it is the same. When we wanted to have modern tools like New Relic, the vendor clearly said no, it is not compatible. After research, I found that there was no product available in the market which could connect the legacy platform to New Relic.

That's where I took the challenge to make this impossible task possible. Then I did a POC and a miracle happened—now it was not only feeding monitoring data to New Relic, but db Internet-specific data in real time, which is precious to the business.

That's how I made 1,000+ services enabled for modern visibility, and it is live with one location. After complete rollout, it can do wonders; it will simplify the legacy landscape and make way for decommissioning db Internet.

This instinct of taking on challenges was not new for me. When db Internet required connecting to the Kannon application for central interest rates, a similar situation arrived.

==============

The connection was simple but coming with a very heavy cost, as it required a specialized authentication system that was not present on the DBI platform. Enabling it and then maintaining it was costing 1 million dollars over 5 years.

I thought, "Can one authentication system really require so much money, or am I just dreaming?" But it turned out it was the cost of that specialist team for the legacy platform who would be maintaining it.

Then I became the specialist—not for legacy, but to save cost. I designed a sidecar architecture on Fabric which has the required setup and can connect to both systems: from Kannon with the required authentication (if I have to tell the specific name, Kerberos) and to DBI via REST.

That's how I saved 1 million dollars for Deutsche Bank, and it is now serving 8 countries. Also, it was designed in a way that it can easily be migrated to the GCP platform.

When I look back, only architecture or challenging the conventional way is not enough; taking the client's perspective is equally important.

==============

When I got a half-baked migration project for db ESA (a product which serves precious metals and Forex shipment for 3 banks), the real problem was not technicalities, but alignment between two major German banks, Commerzbank and ReiseBank, to get the requirements and convert them into a language which the technical team could understand. As they were used to the legacy system, there were many requirements coming through the mindset that "we want a similar process at our end."

After engaging with all stakeholders, translating business requirements to the technical team, I designed an adapter which can understand the language of external banks and translate it to the newly onboarded product called Cinovo (automation).

I led the technical team and successfully completed the migration.

After that, it got sold. Here, I managed to keep the external banks' relationship healthy as well as get higher commercial value for the product, also saved 250k dollars yearly, and decommissioned three legacy applications.

In the db ESA project, I remember one occasion where my team member was trying hard to learn a skill on Fabric deployment, and I helped him learn it quickly.
